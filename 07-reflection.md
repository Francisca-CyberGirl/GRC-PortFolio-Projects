# Short reflection (about 150 words)


*Word count: 145.*


The hardest part was designing for three different working worlds with one small team. Office staff can learn through email and a learning platform, but most warehouse staff have no desk time or email, so an email-and-simulation programme would have reached only about 55% of the company. I chose supervisor-led five-minute huddles and QR codes at kiosks, accepting that sign sheets and observation are weaker evidence than platform data. The main trade-off was proving behaviour change while protecting trust. Phishing simulations give numbers, but the NCSC warns they can erode trust, so I kept them quarterly, aggregated and supportive, and judged them by reports as well as clicks. The biggest uncertainty is the baseline: I cannot know Northbridge’s starting point, so every target is a planning choice to be confirmed in Month 1. Reported incidents may rise first, and leadership should be told in advance.
