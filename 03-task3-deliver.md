# Task 3 – Deliver


## 3.1 Delivery formats


Short, repeated learning in the flow of work, blending modules, simulations, posters and team briefings (ICDFA, n.d., slide 7).


*Table 6. Formats, time per person (planning figures) and fit. Total learning time is about 1,545 staff hours a year.*


| **Audience** | **Format and frequency** | **Hours a year** | **Why it fits** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Warehouse** | Five-minute monthly huddle card led by the supervisor; two ten-minute kiosk modules; posters with QR codes; annual 15-minute refresher | 1.5 | No desk time or email; supervisors already hold huddles; works on a kiosk or paper |
| **General office** | Monthly micro-lesson of 5–8 minutes in the LMS; quarterly simulation; short tips | 1.5 | Fits between tasks; repetition builds habit |
| **Finance and procurement** | 30-minute procedure briefing; three-hour fraud tabletop with an external facilitator; invoice-themed simulation; refresher | 5 | Practises the exact pressure used in invoice fraud |
| **Remote sales** | 45-minute online workshop (two slots); travel and device brief; CRM data refresher; LMS modules | 3 | Recorded and flexible for people on the road |
| **Executives and senior managers** | Two 30-minute briefings; join the fraud tabletop for one hour | 2 | Respects their time; builds sponsorship |
| **IT administrators and security** | Half-day privileged-access lab; three-hour incident drill; LMS modules | 8 | Hands-on practice for people who hold powerful access |


**Onboarding [Proposal].** Day 1: a 15-minute essentials session at account set-up (own password, MFA, how to report), with access activated afterwards. By day 7: a short module for the person’s audience. By day 30: the role session with the next cohort. At day 90: a pulse check. HR tracks completion; the target is 100% by day 7 for the 160 expected joiners (A5).


**Phishing simulation approach [Proposal].** One baseline simulation (Month 1), then quarterly simulations for all email users (Months 3, 6, 9 and 12) and one invoice-themed simulation for finance (Month 7). Lures copy real scams but never use pay, bonus, illness or redundancy themes. Staff are told in policy that simulations occur. Anyone who clicks sees a 30-second explanation at once. Anyone who reports gets a thank-you. People who fail two of the last four simulations are offered a private ten-minute coaching chat with the Awareness Coordinator; line managers are not told, there is no discipline and no league table, and results are shown only in aggregate for groups of ten or more. This follows the NCSC warning that simulations cannot teach everything and can erode trust, so reports matter as much as clicks (NCSC, n.d.). For warehouse staff without email, supervisor-led scenario drills (for example, an unknown “driver” asks for a gate pass) replace covert tests.


**No-blame reporting route [Proposal].** (1) A one-click “Report phishing” button in email. (2) For everyone, including warehouse staff: a QR-code form of four short questions at every kiosk and noticeboard, and a Security Desk phone number; supervisors may report on a worker’s behalf. (3) Human acknowledgement the same working day, within four working hours for payment-related reports, and a follow-up saying what the report led to. (4) A written COO statement: reporting a mistake or suspicious message promptly will never lead to discipline; only deliberately ignoring policy or hiding an incident can. Users who fear reprisals will not report promptly (NCSC, n.d.).
